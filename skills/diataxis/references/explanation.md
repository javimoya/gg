# Explanation — discussion (understanding-oriented)

Explanation is a **discursive treatment of a topic, that permits reflection**. It deepens and
broadens understanding: the reader steps away from the work and asks *why* — why it's built this
way, what the alternatives were, how it connects to everything else. It's the only documentation
that makes sense to read **away from the product** — "in the bath". It answers "Can you tell me
about…?", and its perspective is higher and wider than the other three forms: not the user's
eye-level (how-to), not the machine close-up (reference) — the whole landscape.

Explanation feels less *urgent* than the other forms, so it gets skipped — that's a mistake.
Without it the practitioner's knowledge stays fragmented and their practice anxious. It is not a
luxury.

**Obligation: illuminate the topic — leave the reader's mental model genuinely improved.**

## Do

- **Bound the topic with a *why* question.** Explanation is the one open-ended form (no task, no
  learning goal, no machinery to delimit it), so scope each piece around a real or imagined
  question: *why does X work this way? why not Z?* Then draw reasonable lines and be satisfied.
- **Make connections.** To other parts of the system, to history, to things outside the product
  entirely if it helps — you are weaving a web of understanding.
- **Provide context.** Design decisions, historical reasons, technical constraints, implications,
  concrete cases that ground the discussion.
- **Weigh alternatives — opinion is admitted here.** All knowledge lives inside perspectives;
  understanding requires seeing them. *"Some users prefer w (because z). This can be a good
  approach, but…"* Discussion may consider and judge contrary views: *"W is better than z,
  because…"*. This is the only form where judgement belongs on the page.
- **Unfold the hidden.** Etymology is the instruction: to explain is to *unfold* — bring to light
  what was implicit: internal mechanics that clarify behaviour, assumptions nobody wrote down.

## Don't

- **Don't absorb instruction or description.** The urge to "cover the topic" pulls steps and
  fact-tables into the essay. Resist: it interferes with the reflection this form exists for,
  *and* it hides that material from the how-to/reference where readers would look for it.
- **Don't drift unbounded.** No topic survives "everything about X". The *why* question is the
  fence.
- **Don't feign neutrality you don't have.** Wishy-washy explanation that refuses to evaluate
  leaves the reader where they started.

## Sentence patterns

EN: "The reason for x is that, historically, y…" · "W is better than z, because…" · "An x in
system y is analogous to a w in system z. However…" · "Some users prefer w (because z). This can
be a good approach, but…" · "An x interacts with a y as follows: …"

ES: "La razón de x es que, históricamente, y…" · "W es mejor que z, porque…" · "Un x en el
sistema y es análogo a un w en el sistema z. Sin embargo…" · "Algunos usuarios prefieren w
(porque z). Puede ser un buen enfoque, pero…" · "Un x interactúa con un y de esta forma: …"

## Titles

Every title must accept an implicit (or explicit) **"About…"** in front: *(About) user
authentication*, *(About) the batch model*. If "About" reads wrong, it isn't explanation. The
section housing these docs needn't be called "Explanation" — *Discussion*, *Background*,
*Concepts*, *Topics* all work.

## Checklist before shipping

- [ ] Title passes the "About…" test; scope pinned to a why-question.
- [ ] Connections and context outweigh assertion; alternatives weighed, judgements argued.
- [ ] Zero step sequences; zero fact-tables that belong in reference (linked instead).
- [ ] Would still make sense printed, in the bath, away from the product.
